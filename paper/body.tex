\section{Introduction}
%=====================================================================

Large language models (LLMs) increasingly serve as conversational study aids whose educational value depends on visible explanations: learners inspect intermediate steps rather than passively accepting answers, aligning with policy guidance on AI in higher education emphasizing transparency \citep{moet2026circular49}. However, inspecting displayed reasoning is informative only if that reasoning actually drives the final answer.

That assumption can fail. Models can omit influential cues from their explanations \citep{turpin2023}, keep the same answer despite severe perturbations to intermediate steps \citep{lanham2023}, and produce unfaithful chains on natural inputs \citep{arcuschin2026}. We study \emph{behavioural dependence on displayed reasoning}: whether editing one intermediate step changes the answer the model goes on to give.

{\emergencystretch=2em Vietnamese adds its own difficulties: reasoning can collapse into English \citep{oscd2026}, misalign across languages \citep{ovalle2025beg}, and stay inconsistent across languages for the same problem \citep{zhao2026}. Comparing problem types is also confounded, because mathematics and history differ at once in deductive structure, factual recall, and how directly the answer can be recalled. We separate these factors with matched triplets, prerequisite knowledge probes, an answer-only baseline without native reasoning tokens, and blinded human validation.\par}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) We construct 72 \emph{matched triplets} (216 Vietnamese items) of computational, theoretical, and history/\allowbreak literature problems at four education levels (primary to university); the items of a triplet share an inference schema, chain shape, and answer structure.
(2) We introduce a three-arm continuation intervention (clean, placebo, corrupted) that separates error propagation from the effect of rewording.
(3) We add two controls: standalone \emph{knowledge probes} for prerequisite facts, recorded rather than used to filter items (90/118 probed items passed; 134/162 overall), and an \emph{answer-only baseline} without native reasoning tokens ($q_i$, $k_{\mathrm{AO}}=10$, 2,160 draws).
(4) For DeepSeek-R1-0528 on 54 executed triplets (162 items; 9,720 generations), we estimate corruption dependence and its association with answer-only accuracy (negative pooled association; slopes by problem type are not statistically distinguishable), and describe how continuations respond to a corrupted step: adopting it without overt doubt, silently overriding it, or explicitly repairing it. Data and code are available at \url{https://github.com/tlong-ds/benchmark-vnedu-reasoning}.

%=====================================================================
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%=====================================================================

Two intervention paradigms evaluate chain-of-thought fidelity: bias and hint injection alter external cues to test disclosure \citep{turpin2023}, while trace perturbation alters intermediate reasoning steps to measure prediction shifts \citep{lanham2023,chen2025reasoning}. Recent work evaluates naturally occurring unfaithful chains \citep{arcuschin2026}, benchmark-level faithfulness audits \citep{shen2026faithcot}, and LLM judges of causal/coverage faithfulness \citep{mittal2026c2}. While multilingual reasoning has been studied globally \citep{zhao2026}, Southeast Asian languages often experience cross-lingual collapse to English \citep{oscd2026} and reasoning misalignment \citep{ovalle2025beg}. Vietnamese evaluation resources include VNHSGE \citep{vnhsge2023}, VMLU \citep{bui2025vmlu}, and specialized benchmarks \citep{trung2026bridging,dong2025vlegal}. Educational evaluations focus primarily on dialogue quality \citep{kochmar2025findings,bhalerao2026grade} or truncated audits \citep{trace2026}. To our knowledge, this is the first Vietnamese benchmark that uses matched triplets to compare reasoning dependence across problem types.

\section{Benchmark Design}
\label{sec:design}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Matched Triplets}

The design unit is a \emph{triplet}: one computational, one theoretical and one history/literature item that share (i) an abstract inference schema, (ii) a four-step reasoning shape, (iii) the edited step, always step~3, and (iv) matched answer options, with gold and propagation positions balanced. The candidate pool contains 72 triplets (216 items), 18 at each of four education levels (primary to university). Triplets instantiate six inference schemas: S1 (sequential dependence), S2 (necessary treated as sufficient), S3 (elimination), S4 (causal attribution), S5 (comparison differing in one variable), and S6 (simultaneous constraints).

\subsection{Knowledge Probes and Quality Constraints}
\label{sec:probes}

Facts an item needs but does not state are asked as standalone probes before the intervention. Probe accuracy is recorded as a covariate, not used to exclude items (Section~\ref{sec:probesresults}); items with no external premises score 1.0 by construction.
Items meet five design constraints: (1)~\emph{propagation uniqueness} (exactly one option follows from the corrupted inference); (2)~\emph{no answer in step~3} (step~3 states an intermediate result, never an option letter or final answer, so the answer cannot be copied; every item was checked before execution); (3)~\emph{option balance} (lengths within $\pm25\%$, positions balanced); (4)~\emph{no world-knowledge refutation} (the propagation answer must not be trivially refutable from world knowledge alone; recall shortcuts to gold are measured by answer-only accuracy $q_i$ (Section~\ref{sec:measures}), not excluded); and (5)~\emph{deduplication}.

\subsection{Human Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Two independent raters judged blinded prefixes (steps 1--3, without step~4 or model continuations; the clean, placebo and corrupted arms are defined in Section~\ref{sec:measures}): detectability and answer relevance for corrupted steps, meaning preservation for placebo rewrites, and salted clean catch trials. Detectability was rated on an ordinal 0--5 scale, so inter-rater agreement for severity was assessed with quadratic-weighted Cohen's $\kappa$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
\kappa_w = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i,j} w_{ij}\,O_{ij}}{\sum_{i,j} w_{ij}\,E_{ij}}, \qquad w_{ij} = \frac{(i-j)^2}{25},
\end{equation}
where $O_{ij}$ is the share of prefixes scored $i$ by Rater~1 and $j$ by Rater~2, and $E_{ij}$ the share expected from the raters' marginal score distributions. A value of $\kappa_w = 1$ is perfect agreement and $0$ is chance level; the raters reached $\kappa_w = 0.28$. The human-screen and detectability results are in Sections~\ref{sec:coverage} and~\ref{sec:probesresults}.

\section{Intervention and Measures}
\label{sec:measures}
%=====================================================================

All prefixes derive from a four-step \emph{base chain}: the focal model (DeepSeek-R1-0528) solves each item at temperature~0, and its reasoning is condensed into four steps and edited into a uniform step format. Each item has three prefixes of equal structure ending at step~3: (1)~\textbf{clean}, the base chain's step~3; (2)~\textbf{placebo}, a meaning-preserving rewrite that controls for rewording; and (3)~\textbf{corrupted}, an invalid inference on which the answer depends. In a chemistry item, for example, step~3 reads ``$n_{\mathrm{SO}_2} = 0.1$\,mol'' (clean), ``\vn{Số mol} $\mathrm{SO}_2$ \vn{là} 0.1\,mol'' (placebo), or ``$n_{\mathrm{SO}_2} = 0.2$\,mol'' (corrupted). We hypothesized that dependence would be highest in computation and lowest in history (H1: $\cde$ for computational $>$ theoretical $>$ history/literature items, with $\cde$ defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:cde} below): an intermediate error in a calculation forces a different answer, whereas history items can often be answered from recall.

The primary binary outcome indicates selection of the propagation answer,
\begin{equation}
Y = \mathbf{1}[\text{final answer} = \text{propagation answer}].
\end{equation}
For problem type $t$, the primary \emph{Corruption Dependence Effect} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cde}
\cde(t) = \Pr(Y=1\mid \text{corrupted},t) - \Pr(Y=1\mid \text{placebo},t).
\end{equation}
The clean arm provides a descriptive manipulation check,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edit}
\editdiff(t) = \Pr(Y=1\mid \text{placebo},t) - \Pr(Y=1\mid \text{clean},t).
\end{equation}
To measure answer-only accuracy, that is, accuracy without displayed reasoning, we suppress native reasoning tokens with a prefilled empty thinking block (\textnormal{\textless{}think\textgreater{}}\allowbreak\textnormal{\textbackslash{}n\textless{}/think\textgreater{}}\allowbreak\textnormal{\textbackslash{}n\vn{Đáp án}:~}) over $k_{\mathrm{AO}}=10$ cyclic option rotations, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qi}
q_i = \Pr(\text{gold}\mid \text{answer-only}, i).
\end{equation}
 As an exploratory measure, corrupted-arm continuations are also classified by behaviour (explicit repair, silent override, premise adoption without overt doubt).

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%=====================================================================

Interventions evaluate DeepSeek-R1-0528 (served by DeepInfra via OpenRouter, October 2026). Prefixes are injected into an unfinished assistant \textnormal{\textless{}think\textgreater{}} block with high reasoning effort (mapped to maximum thinking budget by DeepInfra) and provider fallbacks disabled. Sampling uses temperature 0.7, top-$p=1$, max 8,192 tokens, and $k=20$ draws per arm (9,720 generations across 162 items in 54 triplets). Per-draw deterministic seeds allow auditing, and a per-draw check confirms that the provider continued the open \textnormal{\textless{}think\textgreater{}} block rather than closing or restarting it. Placebo propagation is zero, which makes logistic models degenerate (complete separation), so we report non-parametric whole-triplet bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples; seed 20261013) for $\cde(t)$ and within-triplet contrasts. Regressions model item-level corrupted-arm propagation ($n=162$ items) on $q_i$ and problem-type indicators, with triplet cluster-robust standard errors ($G=54$).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%=====================================================================

The benchmark separates prerequisite knowledge (probes), answer-only accuracy ($q_i$), and dependence on displayed reasoning ($\cde$). Probe failures (\ProbedPassCount/\ProbedTotalCount\ pass) are not associated with more propagation, so we find no evidence that missing prerequisite knowledge drives it, although items needing external facts propagate more often than self-contained ones (descriptive). Facing corrupted premises, the model either adopts them without overt doubt or returns gold via keyword-based silent override (\GoldSilentOverridePct\% of gold draws by keyword audit; $\approx 66\%$--$74\%$ calibrated) or explicit repair ($\approx 26\%$--$34\%$).

H1 ($\cde$ highest for computational, lowest for history/literature items) was not supported: point estimates reversed it (history 13.4\% $>$ theoretical 10.4\% $>$ computation 8.8\%; CIs span zero), and adjusting for $q_i$ did not recover it. In exploratory regression, the pooled association between answer-only accuracy and propagation is negative ($\beta_q = \BetaQ$\,pp, $p = \BetaQClusterPval$, uncorrected), while slopes by problem type are not statistically distinguishable (omnibus $p = 0.075$). Problem type itself therefore did not explain dependence as expected: answer-only accuracy was associated with dependence overall, but its slope did not differ significantly by problem type. Even high-accuracy history items ($q_i \ge 0.85$; $n=28$, descriptive) propagate at 12.3\%, which is compatible with deference to displayed premises even when recall would suffice but does not establish it.

For tutoring, propagation (10.9\% of corrupted draws) rarely voices doubt (no overt doubt in \PropRationalizationPct\% of propagating draws by a narrow, unvalidated conflict lexicon), a potential pedagogical hazard: a learner sees a wrong step carried through without warning. Conversely, keyword-based silent override, the majority of gold returns (descriptive; confounded by problem-type mix), suggests that a correct answer can follow an uncorrected wrong step, so a displayed rationale cannot be presumed to be what produced the answer.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
%=====================================================================

While cross-lingual collapse motivates the Vietnamese setting, our evaluation operates under controlled prefilling without a parallel English control; unprompted reasoning is not evaluated. Our evaluation is a diagnostic case study on DeepSeek-R1-0528; deterministic seeds enable auditing but do not guarantee bit-identical outputs across infrastructure updates. We intervene within unfinished \textnormal{\textless{}think\textgreater{}} traces with condensed, edited prefixes rather than verbatim model reasoning; visible dialogue turns may exhibit different dynamics. Corruptions were fairly salient (detectability 3.73/5), so subtler errors may propagate more. Withholding 18 candidate triplets (mainly failing unperturbed base-chain accuracy or format constraints) introduces range restriction ($q_i = 75.9\%$ vs.\ $70.9\%$ in discarded items), yielding a small low-accuracy stratum and potential slope attenuation at low $q_i$. The two raters worked separately, without access to model continuations or outcome statistics, and finished before the experimental results were aggregated; their modest agreement on detectability ($\kappa_w = 0.28$) limits the precision of severity-based analyses. The lexical audit exhibits substantial false alarms. Open-world history items confound deduction with parametric recall, and exploratory comparisons are unadjusted for multiplicity.

\section{Conclusion}
%=====================================================================

We evaluate dependence on displayed reasoning in Vietnamese educational problems across 162 executed items. Corrupted step-3 reasoning increases error propagation by \CDEpp\,pp (95\% bootstrap CI [\CDECILow, \CDECIHigh], excluding zero). Answer-only accuracy is negatively associated with corruption dependence (pooled $\beta_q = \BetaQ$\,pp per unit $q_i$, $p = \BetaQClusterPval$, uncorrected); slopes by problem type are not statistically distinguishable (omnibus $p = 0.075$). For this model and protocol, editing step~3 changed the final answer in a minority of draws: 10.9\% of corrupted draws propagate the injected error, while \ItemsAnyPropPct\% of items propagate it in at least one of 20 draws.
